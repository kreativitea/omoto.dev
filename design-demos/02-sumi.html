The classroom was the management training

2 May 2026 · Eleven minutes

Before any of the engineering, I stood in front of a room of Japanese middle schoolers four times a day and tried to get thirty-five people to want something. Nothing since has been harder, and nothing has transferred more completely.

A lesson plan is a hypothesis about attention. You decide what the room should be able to do in forty-five minutes, you choose the smallest number of moves that get there, and then you watch what actually happens and adjust in real time. Every one-on-one I have ever run is that, with a smaller room.

習うより慣れよ
Teaching is the only job I know where you receive feedback on your communication four times a day, from an audience with no incentive to be polite about it.
— from the piece

The specific thing I took from it: never explain twice the same way. If a person did not follow you, the words were the problem, not the person. Managers who are frustrated at being misunderstood have usually never had thirty-five people demonstrate, simultaneously and visibly, that a sentence did not land.
