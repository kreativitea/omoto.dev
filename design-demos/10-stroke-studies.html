Masthead stroke — eleven studies

Each shown at the size it runs on the site, under the wordmark it sits beneath. 00 is what ships today. Everything below it is built from a centreline, a pressure profile and lengthwise kasure, rather than a hand-fitted outline.

00

Current

What the masthead ships today, for comparison.

Michael Toshiro Omoto

01

Tucked entry, lifted exit

The textbook 一: the tip is tucked back on itself at the start, swells through the middle, and lifts to a point. The most "written" of the set.

Michael Toshiro Omoto

02

Heavy head, dry tail

Lands loaded and leaves dry. Most of the ink is spent in the first third, so the tail is more paper than pigment.

Michael Toshiro Omoto

03

Split tail

The bristles separate as the brush lifts and the mark ends in three prongs. Reads fast.

Michael Toshiro Omoto

04

Rising (右上がり)

The lift to the right that a hand naturally puts into a horizontal stroke. Thin, thick, thin, on a clear diagonal.

Michael Toshiro Omoto

05

Flying white (飛白)

Ink starved the whole way, not just at the end. The paper runs through the middle of the mark in long lengthwise gaps.

Michael Toshiro Omoto

06

Pressed stop (蔵鋒)

Blunt at both ends, weight held even across the whole length. The most formal and the quietest.

Michael Toshiro Omoto

07

Fast and dry

Thin, quick and scratchy — more gesture than ink. Sits lightest under the wordmark.

Michael Toshiro Omoto

08

Loaded and wet

A full brush: fat, saturated, almost no dry break. Pools slightly where it slows.

Michael Toshiro Omoto

09

Side brush (側鋒)

Held at an angle, so one edge is clean and the other frays. Asymmetry is the whole idea.

Michael Toshiro Omoto

10

Doubled back

Two passes, the second not quite on the first — a correction rather than a flourish. The most on-brief for a practice sheet.

Michael Toshiro Omoto
