Hiring a hundred engineers in sixteen months

14 June 2026 · Nine minutes

The number is the least interesting part. A hundred hires in sixteen months sounds like a recruiting story, and it was really an onboarding story: the constraint was never how many offers we could extend, it was how many new people the existing team could absorb per week before the codebase stopped making sense to anybody.

I was the first English-speaking engineering manager at a company that ran in Japanese. That is a translation problem for about two weeks and an organisational design problem forever after. You are not adding English speakers to a Japanese company; you are making the company bilingual, which is a different and much more expensive thing to be.

Every process that existed only in someone’s head had to be written down before it could be translated. The translation was the easy half.

What I’d change: I would have built the bootcamp first, not eleventh, and I would have refused to open a fourth team until the third one had a manager who had already run a team. Both mistakes cost about a quarter each, and both were visible in advance if I had been willing to slow the number down.
