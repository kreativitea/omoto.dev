A system that can’t be subpoenaed

The cheapest way to protect a location history is to never have it leave the phone. That sentence took four years to ship. Every solicitation surface in Maps — the prompts that ask you to rate a place you just visited — used to work by asking a server what you had done recently. The server knew, so the server could be compelled to say. this is the whole argument, really

What we changed

The targeting model moved onto the device. The server stopped answering “where has this person been” and started answering “here are the places within a region, ranked.” The phone does the intersection locally and never reports the result back.

  • Candidate places are fetched by coarse region, not by trajectory.
  • Matching happens client-side against on-device history.
  • Only the interaction is logged — never the reason it was shown.

A geofence warrant asks a company to turn over everyone who was near a place at a time. You cannot comply with a question you have made yourself unable to answer.

The engineering is not the hard part. The hard part is that the metric you are judged on — contributions solicited per user — gets worse for two quarters while the model relearns on a smaller feature set. You need a director who will hold that line, and you need to have written down, in advance, what you expected the dip to be. 

// on-device, never transmitted
val nearby = server.candidatesForRegion(coarseCell)
val visited = localHistory.intersect(nearby)   // stays here
if (visited.isNotEmpty()) prompt(visited.best())

We shipped it. Contribution volume recovered in five months and the legal exposure went to zero, which is a number that does not appear on any dashboard I was reviewed against.
