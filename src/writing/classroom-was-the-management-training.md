Before any of the engineering, I stood in front of a room of Japanese middle schoolers four times
a day and tried to get thirty-five people to want something. Nothing since has been harder, and
nothing has transferred more completely.

A lesson plan is a hypothesis about attention. You decide what the room should be able to do in
forty-five minutes, you choose the smallest number of moves that get there, and then you watch
what actually happens and adjust in real time. Every one-on-one I have ever run is that, with a
smaller room.

  <div class="vertical">習うより慣れよ</div>

    Teaching is the only job I know where you receive feedback on your communication four times a
    day, from an audience with no incentive to be polite about it.
  </blockquote>
</div>

<div class="noted">

The specific thing I took from it: never explain twice the same way. If a person did not follow
you, the words were the problem, not the person. Managers who are frustrated at being misunderstood
have usually never had thirty-five people demonstrate, simultaneously and visibly, that a sentence
did not land.
